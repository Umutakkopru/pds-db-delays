### E-01 No baseline for scenario B (Major, group decision)

- **Finding.** B0, B1 and B2 are all scenario A baselines (trained on A rows with timetable and calendar features). M-B is compared only with M-A and M-A′. The natural baseline for the en-route question is missing: "a train that left its previous stop 6+ minutes late will leave Würzburg 6+ minutes late".
- **Evidence.** Query Q5 on the B training window (3 Nov 2025 – Apr 2026; 23,931 through-train departures with a non-cancelled earlier stop): correlation between the previous-stop delay and the Würzburg departure delay 0.919; the rule above has accuracy 85.3%, precision 77.2%, recall 84.5% (F1 ≈ 0.81). In 5.6% of these rows the train left its previous stop after the planned Würzburg departure, so the outcome is already almost certain at prediction time.
- **Impact.** The brief grades "performance relative to the baseline". A reviewer will ask what LightGBM adds beyond copying the previous delay. Without this row the M-B result cannot be judged, and a high M-B score may be mostly the rule.
- **Proposed fix.** Add a baseline B3 for scenario B (previous-stop delay ≥ 6 min; as a score: the previous-stop delay itself, or a logistic regression on that one feature), evaluated on the same through-train test rows as M-A′ and M-B, and report M-B − B3 with the same paired bootstrap. This extends D13 and D14, so the group decides.
- **Alternative.** Keep the plan and state the rule's score only in the text; weaker.
- **Second check.** The same query on a different period gives the same picture (85.0% agreement). Disclosure: that first run used test-period rows (Jul – Oct 2026); it is an aggregate of raw columns and was not used for any model choice, but see E-10.

### E-02 `has_plan_record` filter depends on the outcome and is not stable over time (Major, group decision)

- **Finding.** Rows without a timetable record exist only because DB's change feed reported something for them. Excluding them (D8) removes departures that are delayed more than twice as often as the rest, and their share grows from the training period to the test period. The documents give only one number ("3.2% in Oct 2026", all rows).
- **Evidence.** Query Q3, planned, non-cancelled train departures:

  | Period | Departures | Without plan record | Share | Delayed, with record | Delayed, without record | Delayed, all |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Jul 2024 – Nov 2025 | 146,351 | 2,063 | 1.41% | 25.83% | 57.6% | 26.28% |
  | Dec 2025 | 8,357 | 224 | 2.68% | 22.67% | 35.3% | 23.01% |
  | Jan – Jun 2026 (A validation) | 48,425 | 2,426 | 5.01% | 22.00% | 45.8% | 23.19% |
  | Jul – Oct 2026 (test) | 34,118 | 2,150 | 6.30% | 23.01% | 54.0% | 24.97% |

  In 2026, 4,302 of the 4,576 such rows are long-distance trains; only 161 carry the replacement flag.
- **Impact.** The filter is defensible (the flag is known only afterwards, D16), but it lowers the test base rate by about two points and removes 6% of test departures, mostly ICE. A reviewer can call this selection on the outcome. The report must show it; today it would go unnoticed.
- **Proposed fix.** Keep the exclusion; write the table above (from `cleaning_counts.csv`, per split) into the Data section; add a second sensitivity check next to the cancellation one: final models scored once on test rows including departures without a plan record (scenario A features exist for them). - **Second check.** Monthly query Q2 shows the same jump (about 70–170 rows per month until Nov 2025, 360–690 from Jan 2026).

### D-05 Check scripts and project conventions (Minor)

- **Finding.** `CLAUDE.md:157` says station, paths and repository id live only in `src/config.py`. `checks/coverage_check.py` hard-codes all three (`:21-26`, `:38-40`); `checks/data_quality_check.py:62` hard-codes the data period. `coverage_check.py` anchors the 12-hour window on the Würzburg **departure** (`:85-86`), the pipeline on the **arrival** (`01_filter_month.sql:40-41`). The script downloads a raw month when it is missing and deletes it afterwards (`:35-43`, `:143-145`), which contradicts "do not download again" style rules for a check. Its docstring says `python coverage_check.py`, the README `python checks/coverage_check.py`. `category_check.py` and `data_quality_check.py` have no `main()`.
- **Proposed fix.** Import `config` (add `src` to the path as `pytest.ini` does), use the arrival anchor, fail with a clear message instead of downloading, align the docstrings.

### D-06 `pct_delayed_6` is not the target's delay rate (Minor)

- **Finding.** In `quality_by_month.csv` the delayed share is an average over all planned departures: cancelled departures and rows without a plan record are in the denominator. `docs/decision_log.md:43` reports it as "Share departing 6+ min late per month: 19.2% – 30.0%".
- **Evidence.** Q2: per month the share among non-cancelled departures is 19.5–31.1%; the CSV values equal the "of all planned" variant (e.g. Jul 2024: 28.3 vs 29.3).
- **Proposed fix.** Compute the share among non-cancelled departures with a plan record, or rename the column and the table row.

### D-07 `run_pipeline.py` (Minor)

- **Finding.** `pytest` runs only immediately before `05_train.py`, and only if that file exists; today the pipeline never runs the tests. Step 01 rewrites three tracked CSVs on every run (with D-03 this always produces a diff).
- **Proposed fix.** Run `pytest` once at the start in every case; keep the second run before step 05.

### E-03 The 6-minute rule is an arrival rule (Minor)

- **Finding.** The documents call 6 minutes "DB's punctuality threshold" for a departure target and justify excluding cancellations "as in DB's punctuality statistic". DB defines punctuality on arrivals: a stop counts as punctual if the planned arrival time is exceeded by less than 6 minutes (deutschebahn.com, "Erläuterung Pünktlichkeitswerte").
- **Impact.** The threshold is a good choice; the wording invites the question "why departures?".
- **Proposed fix.** One sentence in D2 and the report: "We apply DB's 6-minute threshold, which DB defines for arrivals, to departures, because the passenger waiting at Würzburg experiences the departure." No change to the decision. Times in the data have no seconds (Q2: 0 rows), so "≥ 6 min" equals DB's "more than 5:59".

### E-04 Two definitions of "through train" (Minor, group decision)

- **Finding.** D3 defines through trains as trains that arrive and depart again. `CLAUDE.md:98` plans `is_through` as "has an earlier stop".
- **Evidence.** Q4, modelling rows: before 3 Nov 2025 73,275 departures have a planned arrival but only 67,105 have an earlier stop in the data (6,170 differ). In the test period 15,993 vs 15,921 (72 differ).
- **Impact.** With the second definition the "identical through-train subset" depends on data availability, and M-A′ and M-B silently drop 72 test trains for which the earlier stops are missing.
- **Proposed fix.** `is_through` = planned arrival present; a separate `has_prev_stop`. Decide and state whether the 72 rows are scored (LightGBM handles missing features) or excluded and counted.

### E-05 Early departures disappear in December 2025 (Minor)

- **Finding.** The share of departures recorded before their planned time is 1.1–2.4% per month until November 2025 and at most 0.14% from December 2025. The line-format change, the ARV label and the rise of rows without plan record start in the same month.
- **Evidence.** Q2 (column `neg`). The upstream changelog has no entry for December 2025.
- **Impact.** None on the binary target (negative delays are "not delayed"). It does affect the optional regression (MAE) and the documented statement that exactly-on-time departures rise from ~34% to ~42%: part of the rise is early departures now recorded as 0.
- **Proposed fix.** One sentence in D11 and the EDA; if the regression variant is done, clip delays at 0.

### E-06 Impossible delay values (Minor)

- **Finding.** Four modelling rows have a departure delay between −54 and −1,438 minutes (one change time carries the previous day's date; three look shifted by an hour). 17 rows are below −5 minutes.
- **Evidence.** Q2 (N1): NJ 421 on 12 Nov 2024 (−1,438), RE 19071 on 9 Aug 2025 (−59), RB 57979 on 3 Sep 2026 (−59), RB 57969 on 13 Mar 2026 (−54). The maximum is 396 minutes; no value above 12 hours.
- **Impact.** Four wrong labels at most; but the cleaning step has no rule and the regression variant would be distorted.
- **Proposed fix.** In `02_clean`: delays below −30 minutes are invalid; exclude and count them in `cleaning_counts.csv`.

### E-07 `line_id` rule is tied to a date (Minor)

- **Finding.** `CLAUDE.md:104` builds `line_id` from `train_type` + digits "for RE/RB rows before Dec 2025". The format is not a clean function of the date.
- **Evidence.** Q8, modelling rows from 1 Dec 2025: 2,442 have digit-only lines (December 2025 is mixed, 32.7% digit-only; August 2026 again 2.9%); 825 rows before December 2025 already have a prefix. From December 2025, 1,901 rows have type RB with a line starting with RE (the documents mention this for RE10).
- **Proposed fix.** Decide by format, not date: if the line has a letter prefix, use it; if it has digits only, prefix the train type. State which label wins when type and prefix disagree (the line prefix), and verify via `train_number` as already planned.

### E-10 "Test set used once" has no guard (Minor)

- **Finding.** The protocol says nothing changes after seeing test results. In the week-4 list SHAP, error analysis and the dashboard (which replays test-period departures, D15) come before "Final run on the test set, once". Error analysis on test rows followed by any change would break the rule; nothing in the code would stop it. Label statistics of the test period (delay rate 23.0%, ARV share) are already in the documents.
- **Proposed fix.** Write down: features, tuning, SHAP checks, error-analysis code and the dashboard are developed on validation data; the test run is one command (`05_train.py --final`) that writes `reports/results.md` and the dashboard table and refuses to run if they exist. From now on compute no further label statistics on the test period.

### F-02 `train_type` is not forbidden (Minor)

- **Finding.** `CLAUDE.md:106` and D7 say `train_type` is "never a feature" (ARV is 0.2% of training and 13% of test departures). `FORBIDDEN_COLUMNS` does not contain it, nor the raw `line_number` whose format changes.
- **Evidence.** `src/features_config.py:14-41`; with `FEATURES["A"] = ["train_type", "line_number"]` in a scratch clone `pytest` still reports 10 passed, 1 skipped.
- **Proposed fix.** Add `train_type` and `line_number` to `FORBIDDEN_COLUMNS` (models use `service` and `line_id`).

### F-03 Whitelist tests are weak (Minor)

- **Finding.** With empty feature lists the two whitelist tests pass trivially. They match exact names: in a scratch clone the features `dep_delay_min`, `last_stop_delay`, `delay_wuerzburg`, `minutes_late` passed all tests. ## Appendix: queries

All queries ran with DuckDB 1.5.6 on `data/interim/wuerzburg_*.parquet` (and `data/raw/` where noted). Common views:

```sql
CREATE VIEW allrows AS SELECT * FROM read_parquet('data/interim/wuerzburg_*.parquet', union_by_name = true);
CREATE VIEW w AS
  SELECT *, (epoch(departure_change_time) - epoch(departure_planned_time)) / 60 AS dep_delay,
         COALESCE(departure_is_canceled, FALSE) AS canceled
  FROM allrows WHERE row_role = 'wuerzburg';
-- approximate modelling rows (same rules as D13 / D16)
CREATE VIEW m AS
  SELECT * FROM w
  WHERE departure_planned_time IS NOT NULL AND NOT canceled
    AND has_plan_record AND train_type IS DISTINCT FROM 'Bus';
```

**Q1 Basics.** `DESCRIBE allrows`; `SELECT row_role, COUNT(*), COUNT(DISTINCT id) FROM allrows GROUP BY 1`; rows per `source_month`; station names.

**Q2 Per month and edge cases.**

```sql
SELECT strftime(departure_planned_time, '%Y-%m') AS mth, COUNT(*) AS planned,
       ROUND(100 * AVG((dep_delay < 0 AND NOT canceled)::INT), 2) AS neg,
       SUM((NOT has_plan_record)::INT) AS no_plan,
       ROUND(100 * AVG(canceled::INT), 2) AS canc,
       ROUND(100 * AVG(CASE WHEN NOT canceled THEN (dep_delay >= 6)::INT END), 1) AS del_noncanc,
       ROUND(100 * AVG((NOT canceled AND dep_delay >= 6)::INT), 1) AS del_all
FROM w WHERE departure_planned_time IS NOT NULL AND train_type IS DISTINCT FROM 'Bus'
GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY 1;
SELECT * FROM m WHERE dep_delay < -5 ORDER BY dep_delay;                       -- N1
SELECT SUM((second(departure_planned_time) <> 0)::INT), SUM((second(departure_change_time) <> 0)::INT) FROM w;
-- DST: rows planned 00:00-05:00 on the five days; rows whose delay interval crosses 02:00 on those days
```

**Q3 Rows without plan record by period (E-02).**

```sql
SELECT CASE WHEN departure_planned_time < TIMESTAMP '2025-12-01' THEN '1 Jul24-Nov25'
            WHEN departure_planned_time < TIMESTAMP '2026-01-01' THEN '2 Dec25'
            WHEN departure_planned_time < TIMESTAMP '2026-07-01' THEN '3 Jan-Jun26' ELSE '4 Jul-Oct26' END AS p,
       COUNT(*) AS n, SUM((NOT has_plan_record)::INT) AS no_plan,
       ROUND(100 * AVG(CASE WHEN has_plan_record THEN (dep_delay >= 6)::INT END), 2) AS rate_with_plan,
       ROUND(100 * AVG(CASE WHEN NOT has_plan_record THEN (dep_delay >= 6)::INT END), 1) AS rate_no_plan,
       ROUND(100 * AVG((dep_delay >= 6)::INT), 2) AS rate_all
FROM w WHERE departure_planned_time IS NOT NULL AND NOT canceled AND train_type IS DISTINCT FROM 'Bus'
GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY 1;
```

**Q4 Earlier stops (D-04, D-08, E-04).**

```sql
-- ids linked to more than one Würzburg row
SELECT COUNT(*) FROM (SELECT id FROM allrows WHERE row_role = 'earlier_stop' GROUP BY 1 HAVING COUNT(*) > 1);
-- repeated stop number within one Würzburg row

  WHERE row_role = 'earlier_stop' GROUP BY 1, 2 HAVING COUNT(*) > 1);
-- through trains without any earlier stop, by period
WITH e AS (SELECT DISTINCT wuerzburg_id FROM allrows WHERE row_role = 'earlier_stop')
SELECT (departure_planned_time >= TIMESTAMP '2026-07-01') AS test, COUNT(*) AS dep,
       SUM((arrival_planned_time IS NOT NULL)::INT) AS through_arr, SUM((e.wuerzburg_id IS NOT NULL)::INT) AS has_prev
FROM m LEFT JOIN e ON e.wuerzburg_id = m.id GROUP BY 1;
-- month boundary
WITH e AS (SELECT DISTINCT wuerzburg_id FROM allrows WHERE row_role = 'earlier_stop')
SELECT (day(arrival_planned_time) = 1 AND hour(arrival_planned_time) < 6) AS first_hours, COUNT(*), SUM((e.wuerzburg_id IS NULL)::INT)
FROM m LEFT JOIN e ON e.wuerzburg_id = m.id
WHERE arrival_planned_time IS NOT NULL AND departure_planned_time >= TIMESTAMP '2025-11-03' GROUP BY 1;
```

**Q5 Persistence rule on the B training window (E-01).**

```sql
WITH e AS (
  SELECT wuerzburg_id,
         arg_max((epoch(COALESCE(departure_change_time, arrival_change_time))
                - epoch(COALESCE(departure_planned_time, arrival_planned_time))) / 60, train_line_station_num) AS pd,
         arg_max(COALESCE(departure_change_time, arrival_change_time), train_line_station_num) AS pt_actual
  FROM allrows WHERE row_role = 'earlier_stop' AND NOT COALESCE(departure_is_canceled, FALSE) GROUP BY 1)
SELECT COUNT(*) AS n, ROUND(100 * AVG((dep_delay >= 6)::INT), 1) AS base_rate, ROUND(corr(pd, dep_delay), 3) AS corr_delay,
       ROUND(100 * AVG(((pd >= 6)::INT = (dep_delay >= 6)::INT)::INT), 1) AS accuracy_rule,
       ROUND(100.0 * SUM((pd >= 6 AND dep_delay >= 6)::INT) / SUM((pd >= 6)::INT), 1) AS precision_rule,
       ROUND(100.0 * SUM((pd >= 6 AND dep_delay >= 6)::INT) / SUM((dep_delay >= 6)::INT), 1) AS recall_rule,
       ROUND(100 * AVG((pt_actual > m.departure_planned_time)::INT), 1) AS prev_left_after_planned_dep
FROM m JOIN e ON e.wuerzburg_id = m.id
WHERE m.departure_planned_time >= TIMESTAMP '2025-11-03' AND m.departure_planned_time < TIMESTAMP '2026-05-01';
```
